WEEK 3 TASK: ADVANCED DATA ANALYSIS AND VISUALIZATION IN LOGISTICS

This Week 3 report presents an end-to-end exploratory data analysis and visualization study of a hypothetical last-mile logistics operation using Python. The objective is to understand how analytical techniques and carefully selected visual representations can help interpret delivery efficiency, shipping volumes, transportation costs, and service performance. The project extends the logistics theme of the previous internship tasks while remaining a separate, self-contained GitHub repository.

A fully synthetic, reproducible shipment-level dataset of 960 fictional deliveries was generated to represent operations across six delivery zones over a 92-day period. The dataset includes shipment IDs, dispatch dates, delivery distances, weights, parcel counts, service levels, traffic conditions, weather, promised delivery times, actual completion times, and transportation costs. No confidential or real customer records are included.

Using Python libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, and Matplotlib, I calculated descriptive statistics including mean, median, standard deviation, percentiles, shipment counts, on-time delivery rates, average transportation costs, and Pearson correlations. Group-based analyses were conducted to compare delivery zones, traffic intensity, weather conditions, and service levels. Eight visualizations were generated, including a shipment-volume line chart, delivery-time histogram, zone performance bar chart, cost-distance scatter plot, traffic-related SLA box plot, correlation heatmap, zone cost comparison, and service-level performance chart.

The simulated analysis showed an average delivery time of 4.23 hours, an on-time delivery rate of 93.44%, and a mean modeled transportation cost of ₹161.67 per shipment. The visualizations illustrate how distance, congestion, and service commitments are associated with operational metrics within this hypothetical scenario. These relationships were intentionally built into the simulator and are not presented as real-world findings or causal evidence.

The Word report documents the dataset, methodology, Python code, chart selection rationale, analytical insights, proposed business recommendations, limitations, and future validation requirements. The accompanying GitHub project contains the complete generating and analytical scripts, raw simulated data, exported figures, tables, documentation, dependencies, and basic automated tests. Overall, this work demonstrates how reproducible EDA and data visualization can support more informed logistics analysis and decision-making.
